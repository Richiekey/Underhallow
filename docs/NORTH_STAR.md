# 🥕 Underhallow — North Star V1.0

**Document Status:** Founder-approved direction / Draft for foundation phase  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Purpose:** Establish the immutable creative and product direction for Underhallow before technical and gameplay foundation specifications begin.  
**Authority:** This document is the highest-level product and creative source of truth.  
**Development Model:** AI-agent-assisted, human-directed development  
**Initial Platform:** Browser  
**Initial Mode:** Single-player  

---

## 1. Executive Definition
Underhallow is a cozy, open-ended, isometric pixel-art world where players build their own lives on a mysterious island — farming, hunting, building, exploring, trading, developing skills, meeting characters, and gradually uncovering a deeper story hidden beneath an otherwise peaceful world.

Underhallow should feel like a real game first.  
It should be enjoyable without requiring competition, cryptocurrency, blockchain participation, or optimization.  
Players who want a relaxing experience should be able to relax.  
Players who want to explore should have things to discover.  
Players who want to become wealthy should have meaningful economic opportunities.  
Players who want to compete should have optional competitive trails.  
Players who want to uncover the story should eventually discover that the peaceful world they entered is considerably stranger than it first appeared.  
The long-term vision is a world that players become unexpectedly attached to.

---

## 2. The Player Promise
When someone starts Underhallow, the game promises them:
- **Freedom:** There is no single prescribed way to play.
- **Comfort:** The world is welcoming, cozy, peaceful and rewarding.
- **Discovery:** There are always reasons to explore a little further.
- **Progression:** The player's actions should gradually make their character, home, island and capabilities more meaningful.
- **Ownership:** The player's personal island should eventually feel genuinely theirs.
- **Mystery:** Something deeper exists beneath the surface of the world.
- **Possibility:** Players should be able to imagine what kind of life they want to build.

---

## 3. The Core Player Fantasy
The player enters the world as a new resident.  
They begin with limited resources and knowledge.  
Over time, they build a life.  

They might become:
- A farmer
- A hunter
- A builder
- An explorer
- A trader
- A collector
- A combination of several paths

The game does not force the player to select one permanent identity.  
Instead:
- The player's identity emerges from how they choose to spend their time.
- A player who spends their first 100 hours farming should have a different experience from someone who spends those hours exploring, hunting, building or pursuing wealth.

---

## 4. The Three Primary Gameplay Pillars
The initial game is built around three major activities.

### 4.1 Farming
Players cultivate their personal land, grow crops and develop their agricultural capabilities.  
Farming is one of the game's major progression paths but is not the entirety of the game.

### 4.2 Hunting
Players explore the world, encounter wildlife and other creatures, gather resources and engage in simple combat.  
Hunting provides:
- Exploration incentives
- Resource acquisition
- Skill progression
- Risk/reward
- A second major gameplay path outside farming

Combat should remain approachable and supportive of the broader game rather than turning Underhallow into a combat-first RPG.

### 4.3 Building
Players develop their personal island and home.  
Building includes:
- Houses
- Functional buildings
- Farms
- Paths
- Trees
- Decorations
- Specialized areas
- Future structures and systems

Building should ultimately provide a meaningful expression of player identity.

---

## 5. Secondary Playstyles
The three primary pillars support additional player identities.  
Potential secondary paths include:
- Exploration
- Trading
- Crafting
- Collecting
- Social/community play
- Specialized professions
- Other systems discovered during development

These should initially be treated as playstyles rather than rigid classes.  
A player should be able to transition naturally between activities.

---

## 7. The Main Island
The Main Island is the communal heart of Underhallow.  
It is a medium-sized open world designed around exploration, NPCs, activities, narrative and eventual social interaction.  
It should feel like a coherent place rather than a collection of gameplay screens.  

Potential regions include:
- Town Square
- Residential areas
- Markets
- Farms
- Forests
- Wilderness
- Hunting areas
- Hidden locations
- Coastline
- Harbor
- Other regions discovered during development

The exact geography is intentionally not yet locked.

---

## 8. The Town Square
The Town Square should serve as one of the most important locations in the game.  
It is the player's primary introduction to the social world.  

Potential functions include:
- NPC interactions
- Shops
- Market
- Quest/activity boards
- Community events
- Important buildings
- Story progression
- Seasonal events
- Player gathering
- Future multiplayer activity

The Town Square should feel alive.  
It should contain small environmental details that reward simply walking around and observing the world.

---

## 9. The Personal Island
Every player receives their own persistent island.  
All players initially begin with the same fundamental island layout.  
What happens afterward is up to the player.  

The island is intended to function simultaneously as:
- Farm
- Home
- Building space
- Progression space
- Personal sandbox
- Long-term player property

The island should feel like a genuine place rather than a small farming screen.

---

## 10. Island Expansion
The Personal Island is organized into unlockable grids.  
Players begin with a limited area around their home.  
Additional grids can eventually be unlocked.  
The player determines how newly available land is used.  

Potential uses include:
- Farming
- Buildings
- Trees
- Paths
- Decoration
- Production
- Storage
- Specialized areas
- Future gameplay systems

**Not yet locked:**  
The following require future design:
- Grid dimensions
- Number of initial grids
- Expansion limits
- Unlock requirements
- Pricing
- Whether grids can be purchased
- Whether grids have different properties
- Whether expansion is permanent

These decisions belong in the Foundation Specifications and later economy design.

---

## 11. Player Islands and Expression
Two players should be capable of starting with essentially the same island and eventually producing radically different results.  
One player might create:
- A highly optimized agricultural operation.
- Another: A peaceful forest retreat.
- Another: A dense village-like settlement.
- Another: A trading and production center.
- Another: A beautiful decorative garden.

The island should therefore become an important form of player expression.

---

## 12. Visiting Other Players
Multiplayer is not part of the initial game.  
However, the world should be designed with eventual social interaction in mind.  
The long-term vision includes players being able to visit another player's island.  

Initial envisioned interactions include:
- Walking around
- Exploring
- Socializing
- Leaving gifts

Future interactions may be introduced if they are technically and mechanically appropriate.  
Multiplayer must not compromise the initial single-player experience.

---

## 13. Exploration
Exploration is a major pillar of the world.  
The player should regularly encounter reasons to investigate their surroundings.  
The world may contain:
- Hidden areas
- Rare resources
- NPCs
- Secrets
- Rare items
- Lore
- Quests
- Environmental clues
- Unusual encounters
- Hidden pathways
- Future discoverable systems

The goal is to create moments where the player thinks: *"What's over there?"* and is rewarded for finding out.

---

## 14. Progression
Underhallow should use multi-dimensional progression.  
Current intended progression dimensions include:
- **Character:** Character level, general progression.
- **Skills:** Farming, hunting, building.
- **Property:** House, island, buildings, land/grid expansion.
- **Economy:** Wealth, trading capability, economic opportunities.
- **Discovery:** Potential future progression through exploration, collections, lore, discoveries.
- **Relationships:** Potential future progression through NPC relationships, reputation, community standing.

The exact progression architecture is intentionally deferred.

---

## 15. Success and Failure
Underhallow should not define success through one metric.  
A player can succeed by:
- Becoming a master farmer
- Becoming an accomplished hunter
- Building a beautiful island
- Becoming wealthy
- Discovering secrets
- Completing character stories
- Developing relationships
- Collecting rare items
- Exploring the world
- Mastering multiple professions

The game should support different definitions of a successful playthrough.

---

## 16. Endgame Philosophy
Underhallow does not require a traditional final ending.  
Players can complete major milestones and story arcs, but the world continues afterward.  

The intended philosophy is:  
*Stories can conclude. The player's life does not have to.*  

The player should be able to continue farming, building, hunting, exploring, trading, collecting, developing their island, and pursuing optional challenges after completing major narrative milestones.

---

## 17. The Economy
The long-term vision includes a meaningful player economy.  
The economic fantasy is:  
*Players should have the opportunity to build wealth through effort, skill, strategy, opportunity and sometimes luck.*  

The economy should accommodate different strategies:
- Farming
- Trading
- Production
- Rare discoveries
- Market opportunities
- Specialized professions
- Other systems developed later

However:  
Wealth is not the only form of success.  
Players should never be forced to participate in the economic game to enjoy Underhallow.

---

## 18. Real-World Economic Vision
The long-term Underhallow vision includes the possibility of an economy connected to real-world economic value.  
However, this is a future objective, not an immediate implementation requirement.  

The economic system must first be designed as a compelling game economy.  
Only afterward should we determine:
- Whether blockchain is appropriate
- What assets should be on-chain
- How real-world value interacts with the game
- How economic risks are handled
- How players are protected
- How the economy avoids becoming pay-to-win
- Regulatory and operational implications

No blockchain implementation is locked into V1.0.

---

## 19. Web3 Philosophy
Underhallow should work as a normal game.  
A player should be able to enjoy Underhallow without owning cryptocurrency, connecting a wallet or understanding blockchain technology.  
Blockchain, if eventually used, should enhance the game's economy rather than define the game's identity.  
The game comes first.

---

## 20. Competition
Competition is optional.  
Underhallow should not pressure players into constantly comparing themselves against others.  
However, players who enjoy competition should have optional competitive trails/challenges (farming achievements, hunting achievements, exploration challenges, wealth challenges, building competitions, seasonal challenges, collection challenges, time-based challenges).  
Competitive systems should complement the cozy experience rather than dominate it.

---

## 21. Narrative Identity
The narrative is a major component of Underhallow.  
The player arrives as a new resident in an apparently ordinary, peaceful world.  
At first, everything feels safe and welcoming.  
The player gradually encounters characters, institutions, events and discoveries that suggest that something about the world is not quite right.  
The mystery should develop slowly.  
The game should not immediately announce: *"Something is wrong."*  
Instead, players should eventually realize it themselves.

---

## 22. The Mystery
The desired narrative experience is:
- **First impression:** *"This is a cute farming game."*
- **After some time:** *"Hmm. That's strange."*
- **Later:** *"Wait..."*
- **Eventually:** *"WHAT THE FUCK IS GOING ON?"*

The contrast between the cozy surface and the deeper mystery is an intentional part of Underhallow's identity.  
The mystery should make players reinterpret things they encountered earlier.

---

## 23. Narrative Tone
The game operates across two simultaneous layers:
- **Surface Layer:** Cozy, wholesome, relaxing, friendly, funny, peaceful, adventurous.
- **Underlying Layer:** Mysterious, strange, unexplained, occasionally unsettling, deeply curious, potentially dark.

The darker layer should not make the game unpleasant to play.  
The player should be able to enjoy an ordinary farming session while still sensing that something larger exists beneath the surface.

---

## 24. Existing Narrative Continuity
The previous Underhallow development established useful narrative foundations around:
- The Valley
- The new farmer
- Clementine
- Robinhood
- The Department of Agriculture
- Institutional absurdity
- Strange agricultural phenomena
- Hidden history
- A mystery that is never immediately explained

These concepts are considered valuable source material for the new narrative direction, but the previous gameplay systems and economic implementation are not automatically carried forward.  
The new Foundation Specifications will determine what survives, changes or gets replaced.

---

## 25. Character Philosophy
Characters should feel like real inhabitants rather than quest dispensers.  
NPCs should have: personalities, relationships, motivations, histories, opinions, routines, character arcs, and connections to the wider world.  
The player should eventually become attached to certain characters.  
Character-driven storytelling should be one of the primary mechanisms through which the larger story is revealed.

---

## 26. Visual Identity
The definitive visual direction is:  
**Isometric Pixel Art**  
The world should create the illusion of three-dimensional space while remaining fundamentally pixel-art driven.  
- Isometric/3⁄4 perspective
- Cozy architecture
- Dense but readable environments
- Warm colors
- Detailed buildings
- Paved streets
- Markets
- Trees and vegetation
- Environmental props
- Strong visual depth
- Nostalgic pixel-art feeling

The reference is a directional benchmark, not a literal asset template.

---

## 27. Art Philosophy
The visual goal is: **Simple, cohesive, recognizable and beautiful.**  
We prioritize consistency over excessive detail.  
A smaller number of highly cohesive assets is preferable to a huge collection of visually inconsistent assets.  
The world should feel as though it was created by one artistic vision.

---

## 28. Nostalgia
The visual experience should evoke the feeling of classic 16-bit RPGs, simulation games, farming games, and adventure games without simply copying any existing game.  
The goal is nostalgia for a game that feels familiar but is unmistakably Underhallow.

---

## 29. World Design Philosophy
The Main Island should feel geographically and visually coherent.  
Places should have reasons to exist. Forests should connect logically to wilderness. Rivers should make geographical sense. Settlements should have understandable relationships with roads, farms and natural resources.  
The world should feel designed as a place rather than assembled as a collection of game mechanics.

---

## 30. Platform
The initial platform is **Browser**.  
The first version should prioritize:
- Fast startup
- Strong performance (60 FPS)
- Reliable controls (keyboard/mouse + gamepad compatibility)
- Good rendering performance
- Reasonable asset sizes
- Browser compatibility
- Persistent game state

---

## 31. Multiplayer Strategy
Initial development is **Single-player first**.  
The first playable game should not require multiplayer infrastructure.  
However, the architecture should avoid making future multiplayer unnecessarily impossible:  
*Don't build multiplayer now. Don't accidentally architect ourselves into a dead end either.*

---

## 32. Development Philosophy
Underhallow is ambitious enough to have long-term potential but constrained enough that the first version must be realistically finishable.  
Target: **Medium-sized, polished, genuinely playable.**  
We will build a small, high-quality core and expand from there.

---

## 33. The First Success Criterion
The first major milestone is not feature count, map count, items, NPCs, or economic complexity.  
The first milestone is:  
**The game is fun to play and visually feels like Underhallow.**

---

## 34. First Playable Experience
The first prototype should demonstrate the fundamental fantasy loop:
```
Enter the world
      ↓
Explore the Main Island
      ↓
Discover the Town
      ↓
Meet an NPC
      ↓
Receive an objective
      ↓
Explore / gather
      ↓
Travel to Personal Island
      ↓
Farm
      ↓
Harvest
      ↓
Build / customize
      ↓
Return to the Main Island
      ↓
Continue exploring
```
If this loop is not enjoyable, we stop and improve it before expanding the scope.

---

## 35. What Underhallow Is NOT
Underhallow is not intended to become:
- A leaderboard-first game
- A blockchain-first game
- A wallet-first game
- A farming spreadsheet
- A pure idle game
- A combat-first RPG
- A linear story game
- A game where players must compete to progress
- A game where monetization dictates gameplay
- An enormous MMO on day one
- A collection of disconnected mechanics
- An AI-generated world without coherent artistic direction

**Most importantly:** Underhallow must never become more interested in its systems than in whether the player is actually having fun.

---

## 36. Long-Term Multiplayer Vision
Multiplayer is a future layer (island visits, gifts, coop activities, events, markets) and intentionally a future possibility, not a V1 commitment.

---

## 37. Long-Term Economic Vision
A functioning in-game economy that is interesting even before it has any relationship with real-world value. Rewards work, knowledge, timing, risk management, specialization, exploration, and luck.

---

## 38. AI-Agent Development Philosophy
Underhallow will be developed using multiple specialized AI agents. Agents are collaborators and implementation systems, not product owners.

---

## 39. Agent Autonomy
Agents receive controlled autonomy within approved scopes (research, inspect, identify problems, design details, write code, refactor, test, optimize, recommend).  
Agents may not independently redefine foundational product decisions.

## 41. Changing the North Star
Agents may identify problems and recommend changes with evidence. Agents may NOT silently change the North Star. Changes require founder-level approval.

---

## 42. Agent Planning Philosophy
- **Small task:** Inspect → implement → test → report.
- **Significant task:** Research → inspect relevant systems → identify constraints → propose approach → implement → test → verify → report.
- **Architectural task:** Audit → identify dependencies → produce an explicit architecture plan → receive appropriate approval → implement → run comprehensive verification.

---

## 43. Human QA
Human QA is the ultimate gate for fun, feel, visual quality, game pacing, player experience, art cohesion, narrative impact, and whether something actually feels like Underhallow.

---

## 44. The Five-Year Vision
A genuinely fun game that people didn't expect to become so attached to. It feels like a real game first.

---

## 45. The Underhallow Principle
*Does this make Underhallow a better game?*  
If the answer is no, it does not belong.

---

## 46. North Star Summary
- Cozy, open-ended, isometric pixel-art game.
- Dual-world structure: Shared Main Island (hub/social/exploration) + Personal Island (player-owned farm/build/expand).
- Core loop: Farm → Hunt → Build → Explore → Trade → Progress → Discover.
- Peaceful surface layer with a deep, unsettling mystery underneath.
- Browser-first, single-player first, fun and polish before scale.

## 48. Document Authority
This document establishes what Underhallow is trying to become.  
It does not specify exactly how the game will be implemented. That distinction is intentional.  

The development sequence:
1. **North Star V1.0** → Vision *(This Document)*
2. **Foundation Specification** → Product/system definition
3. **Technical Architecture** → Implementation architecture
4. **Agent Constitution** → How AI agents execute the work
5. **Production Plan** → What gets built, in what order, and how we verify it

*Only after those layers are established should we begin rebuilding the game from scratch.*
