## 1. Creative North Star

**Underhallow is a cozy, open-ended life game set inside a beautiful mystical world that hides a deeper mystery.**

The player arrives as a newcomer who has inherited a piece of land in a seemingly peaceful community.

They can farm, hunt, explore, build, fish, craft, trade, meet people, improve their home, and gradually build a life of their own.

At first, Underhallow feels like a place where the player can simply settle down.

But the longer they stay, the more they realize:

**the world they inherited is not as simple as it appears.**

The residents know things the newcomer does not.

Places have histories.

People have secrets.

The environment contains clues.

And eventually, the seemingly ordinary life the player has built becomes connected to something much larger.

The mystery should never prevent the player from enjoying their life.

**Discovery is an invitation, not an obligation.**

---

# 2. The Player's Emotional Journey

Underhallow should consistently evoke five primary emotions:

1. **Cozy**
2. **Freedom**
3. **Curiosity**
4. **Adventure**
5. **Mystery**

The intended emotional balance is approximately:

**65% Cozy / 35% Adventure**

However, mystery should exist underneath both.

The player should be able to spend an entire session simply farming, decorating their home, hunting, fishing, exploring, or interacting with residents without feeling that they are failing to advance the story.

At the same time, the world should constantly communicate:

> *There is more here if you want to look.*

---

# 3. The 50-Hour Feeling

After dozens of hours, the player should be able to stop somewhere on their island, look around, and think:

> **"This is my little world. I built this."**

Their house, land, crops, structures, possessions, relationships, discoveries, and memories should make the world feel personally owned.

But that feeling should coexist with another:

> **"Wait... what else is out there?"**

The player should never feel that they have completely exhausted the world simply because they have completed the obvious content.

Underhallow should reward players who become curious.

A strange object.

A suspicious NPC.

An unusual location.

A path that was previously inaccessible.

A strange event.

A piece of dialogue that suddenly makes sense much later.

The player should be able to connect these things themselves.

---

# 4. World Identity

Underhallow is a **mystical fantasy world**.

Magic exists, but it is not so commonplace that the world feels like a conventional high-fantasy setting.

The world should still feel tangible and naturally occurring.

Its geography should feel like a **huge natural world**, rather than a collection of disconnected game levels.

Mountains should feel like mountains.

Forests should feel like forests.

Coasts should feel like coasts.

Settlements should feel as though people actually chose to live there.

The islands should contain natural variation, hidden places, ecological relationships, old structures, unexplained locations, and areas that encourage exploration.

The fantastical elements should feel as though they belong to the world rather than being pasted onto it.

---

# 5. The Mystery

Mystery is one of Underhallow's defining creative pillars.

The central mystery should be:

* significant,
* slow to reveal itself,
* connected to the world,
* connected to the inhabitants,
* connected to the player's arrival,
* and eventually central to the overarching story.

The player should initially understand very little.

They are the newcomer.

Everyone else has context.

People may know more than they say.

Some characters may deliberately avoid certain subjects.

Others may simply consider strange things to be normal.

The player gradually pieces together the truth through:

* NPC conversations
* quests
* environmental storytelling
* exploration
* discoverable objects
* unusual events
* locations
* rumors and gossip
* fragments of history
* changes in the world

The game should **not constantly announce the mystery**.

Instead, the player should occasionally notice something that makes them think:

> *"Wait... that's weird."*

Then, many hours later:

> *"Oh."*

And eventually:

> *"So that's what was happening."*

---

# 6. The Player Character

The player inherits a piece of land and arrives as a newcomer.

Their past exists, but it should remain deliberately vague.

They are not a completely empty character, but neither are they a predefined protagonist with a rigid personality.

This allows players to decide who they become through their actions.

The player can become:

* a farmer,
* a hunter,
* an explorer,
* a builder,
* a trader,
* or a mixture of these identities.

Their life should emerge from their choices rather than from a predetermined personality.

---

# 7. The House

The player's house is more than a functional building.

It is a **central symbol of progression**.

At the beginning, it represents what the player inherited.

Over time, it becomes evidence of what they have built.

Its evolution should communicate:

**stranger → resident → homeowner → established member of the world**

The house should therefore become one of the strongest visual representations of the player's journey.

The player should be able to look at it and see their own history.

# 8. Art Direction

## Core Style

Underhallow should use:

**Highly detailed modern pixel art grounded in the visual language of classic SNES-era pixel games.**

The goal is not to imitate SNES limitations literally.

Instead, Underhallow should capture the strengths of that era:

* strong silhouettes,
* readable sprites,
* deliberate pixel placement,
* expressive environments,
* strong composition,
* memorable color relationships,
* and a distinctly handcrafted feeling.

Modern pixel-art detail can then be layered on top.

The result should feel:

**retro at its foundation, modern in execution.**

---

# 9. Environment Detail

Environmental detail should be **moderate**.

The world should feel rich without becoming visually noisy.

Objects should exist because they help communicate:

* place,
* function,
* atmosphere,
* history,
* gameplay,
* or story.

Every environment should have enough detail to reward looking around, but enough visual hierarchy that important objects remain immediately readable.

---

# 10. Readability vs Atmosphere

Readability and beauty have **equal priority**.

The game should never sacrifice gameplay clarity simply to create visual spectacle.

Likewise, environments should never become sterile or overly simplified merely for readability.

The art direction should therefore use:

* strong silhouettes,
* clear interaction cues,
* controlled visual density,
* deliberate contrast,
* environmental grouping,
* recognizable landmarks.

The player should understand what they are looking at while still feeling that the world is beautiful.

---

# 11. Color Language

The core palette should be built around:

* natural greens,
* warm yellows,
* earthy browns,
* soft blues,
* gentle pastels,
* warm sunset oranges,
* deep nighttime blues and purples.

The overall world should feel warm and inviting during ordinary gameplay.

However, color should also become a storytelling tool.

Certain locations, events, creatures, or mysterious phenomena may gradually introduce visual tones that feel subtly different from the ordinary world.

The player should be able to sense that something is wrong before they necessarily understand why.

---

# 12. Biome Identity

Every biome should be immediately recognizable.

Biomes should share a coherent Underhallow visual identity while developing their own:

* color relationships,
* vegetation,
* architecture,
* wildlife,
* lighting,
* environmental hazards,
* landmarks,
* atmosphere.

The player should be able to identify where they are from the environment alone.

---

# 13. Character Design

Characters should use a **proportionate but stylized pixel-art approach**.

They should not be excessively chibi.

They should retain enough human proportion to allow personality, age, occupation, and identity to communicate visually.

Characters should have:

* recognizable silhouettes,
* distinct clothing,
* clear occupations,
* memorable accessories,
* controlled color palettes,
* moderate facial expressiveness.

Because Underhallow will rely heavily on mystery and a relatively focused cast, individual characters should be highly recognizable.

A player should be able to identify an important NPC from their silhouette or clothing even before seeing their name.

---

# 14. Character Expression

Character expressiveness should be **moderate**.

Characters should communicate emotion through:

* facial expressions,
* posture,
* animation,
* body language,
* movement,
* dialogue.

They should not require exaggerated cartoon expressions to communicate personality.

Humor should often come from **character behavior and dialogue**, rather than purely visual comedy.

---

# 15. Narrative Identity

Underhallow's story should explore several connected ideas:

### Build a Life

The player creates something that belongs to them.

### Discover the World

The world should reward curiosity and exploration.

### Become Who You Want to Be

The player determines their identity through their actions.

### There Is More Than You Realize

The world contains layers beneath its peaceful surface.

### Build Something That Lasts

The player's home, relationships, discoveries, and contributions should feel meaningful.

These themes should reinforce one another rather than existing as separate storylines.

---

# 16. Story Prominence

The story should have a **moderate presence with a clear overarching narrative**.

Underhallow should not become a linear story game.

The player should be able to live their life without constantly being pushed toward the next story objective.

However, there should be an unmistakable overarching mystery that gradually becomes more important.

The ideal structure is:

**Life → Curiosity → Discovery → Suspicion → Understanding → Consequence**

---

# 17. Lore Delivery

Lore should primarily be delivered through:

1. **NPC dialogue**
2. **Quests**
3. **Environmental storytelling**
4. **Discoverable objects**
5. **Exploration**
6. **Rumors and gossip**
7. **World events**
8. **Letters, books, and written material**
9. **Item descriptions**
10. **Cutscenes**

Cutscenes should therefore be used selectively.

The world itself should do much of the storytelling.

A player who ignores lore can still enjoy Underhallow.

A player who investigates everything should discover an entirely deeper version of the world.

---

# 18. Tone

The primary tonal combination is:

**Cozy + Mysterious + Adventurous + Wholesome**

The world should feel safe enough to live in.

Interesting enough to explore.

Strange enough to investigate.

And occasionally disturbing enough to make the player question their assumptions.

The contrast is intentional.

The cozy surface makes the darker discoveries more impactful.

---

# 19. Darkness

Underhallow is allowed to contain **genuinely serious moments**.

The world should occasionally challenge the player's assumption that everything is harmless.

However, darkness should be used deliberately.

It should not turn Underhallow into a horror game.

Instead, the contrast should create moments where the player thinks:

> **"I didn't expect this game to go there."**

This contrast is a core part of the game's identity.

---

# 20. Humor

Characters should often be funny, but Underhallow should never become a comedy.

Humor should emerge naturally from:

* personalities,
* relationships,
* misunderstandings,
* village life,
* strange situations,
* NPC habits,
* dialogue.

The world can be charming without constantly trying to make the player laugh.

---

# 21. The Town

The community should initially appear to be an ordinary, charming place.

People have routines.

They work.

They socialize.

They trade.

They farm.

They hunt.

They gossip.

They celebrate.

They argue.

They help each other.

The newcomer sees a functioning community.

But the player gradually realizes that the residents possess knowledge about the world that they do not.

The town should therefore function on two levels:

**What the newcomer sees.**

and

**What the residents already know.**

---

# 22. The Central Creative Contrast

Underhallow should constantly play with the contrast between:

| Surface | Beneath |
| :--- | :--- |
| Cozy | Unsettling |
| Peaceful | Complicated |
| Familiar | Strange |
| Wholesome | Serious |
| Beautiful | Mysterious |
| Ordinary life | Hidden history |
| Small community | Larger world |
| Farming | Discovery |
| Home | Secrets |

This contrast is one of the game's strongest creative tools.

---

# 23. The Player's Core Fantasy

The player's fundamental fantasy is:

> **"I inherited a small piece of this strange world, and I get to decide what kind of life I build there."**

But underneath that fantasy:

> **"The more I learn about this place, the more I realize I inherited more than land."**

---

# 24. Underhallow in One Sentence

**Underhallow is a cozy mystical life game where a newcomer builds a life through farming, hunting, exploration, and other pursuits while gradually uncovering the deeper mystery hidden beneath a seemingly peaceful community.**

---

# 25. The Player's Elevator Pitch

> **"It's basically a farming game, but you'd never guess what's underneath it all."**

This should remain one of the simplest descriptions of Underhallow.

It communicates the fundamental creative contradiction:

**The player thinks they know what kind of game they are playing.**

Then Underhallow gradually proves them wrong.

---

# 26. What Underhallow Should Be Remembered For

Underhallow should ultimately be remembered as:

**the cozy game that had much more underneath the surface than players expected.**

The goal is for players to remember:

* the little home they built,
* the places they discovered,
* the characters they met,
* the strange things they noticed,
* the moments when something suddenly made sense,
* and the realization that the world was much bigger and stranger than it first appeared.

---

# 27. Creative Rule

The most important creative rule for Underhallow is:

> **Never let the mystery destroy the cozy life. Never let the cozy life erase the mystery.**

Both must coexist.

The player should always be able to choose:

**"I want to spend today farming."**

or:

**"I need to find out what the hell is going on."**

Both are legitimate ways to play Underhallow.
