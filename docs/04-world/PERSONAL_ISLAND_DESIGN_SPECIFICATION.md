# 1. Purpose

The Personal Island Design Specification defines the identity, geography, spatial structure, progression, personalization, construction, farming, wildlife, narrative role, and multiplayer boundaries of the player's Personal Island.

The Personal Island is one of the three primary world spaces of Underhallow.

It is the player's:

* home;
* private sanctuary;
* primary farming space;
* personal building space;
* long-term development space;
* place of preparation;
* place of storage;
* expression of identity;
* physical record of their journey.

The Personal Island must not feel like a menu disguised as a map.

It must feel like a **real place that gradually becomes the player's own**.

---

# 2. Design North Star

> **The Personal Island of Underhallow should feel like a home the player slowly creates and grows into rather than a farm they are simply trying to optimize.**

The player should eventually be able to look across their island and think:

> **“This is my home.”**

And, ideally:

> **“I remember when this was empty.”**

The island should physically communicate the player's history.

The player's:

* first crops;
* first building;
* first animal;
* first workshop;
* first major discovery;
* favorite decorations;
* relationships;
* achievements;
* mistakes;
* changes in lifestyle;

should be capable of leaving visible traces.

---

# 3. System Position

The Personal Island is a **Level 2 world/gameplay specification**.

It specializes the universal Building & Construction System defined by **BI-001**.

PI-001 defines:

* where building is permitted;
* what the Personal Island is for;
* how its land is structured;
* how expansion works;
* how the house progresses;
* how farming fits into the island;
* how nature is preserved;
* how wildlife interacts with the space;
* how personalization works;
* how the island participates in progression;
* how the island relates to the Main Island;
* how the island participates in the game's narrative.

BI-001 remains authoritative for universal construction mechanics.

PI-001 must not redefine:

* universal placement validation;
* universal construction lifecycle;
* authoritative building state;
* construction commands;
* generic relocation;
* generic demolition;
* universal material recovery;
* universal building object definitions.

Instead:

> **BI-001 defines how building works. PI-001 defines what building means on a Personal Island.**

---

# 4. World Role

Underhallow has three primary spatial identities:

| Space               | Primary Question              |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Main Island**     | “What is out there?”          |
| **Personal Island** | “This is mine.”               |
| **Guild Islands**   | “What can we build together?” |

The Personal Island therefore provides a deliberate contrast to the Main Island.

The Main Island emphasizes:

* discovery;
* community;
* history;
* danger;
* mystery;
* exploration;
* relationships;
* regional identity.

The Personal Island emphasizes:

* home;
* ownership;
* stability;
* creativity;
* farming;
* building;
* preparation;
* reflection;
* belonging.

Neither space should replace the other.

---

# 5. Emotional Purpose

The Personal Island is the emotional destination of the player's daily loop.

The player may:

* leave home;
* travel to the Main Island;
* explore;
* hunt;
* fish;
* gather;
* complete quests;
* encounter danger;
* discover something strange;
* return home.

The return should feel meaningful.

The intended emotional sequence is:

> **Departure → adventure → uncertainty → accomplishment → return → safety → development → anticipation**

Home should provide relief without becoming boring.

The island should feel especially comforting after difficult expeditions.

---

# 6. Core Identity

The Personal Island is simultaneously:

* a private homestead;
* a personal sanctuary;
* a farm;
* a building canvas;
* a natural environment;
* a progression space;
* a place for animals;
* a storage and preparation base;
* a reflection of player identity.

However, its strongest identity is:

> **A place that gradually becomes yours.**

The island should never feel completely finished.

Even when fully developed, it should contain:

* natural spaces;
* personal choices;
* evolving decorations;
* living animals;
* growing crops;
* functional buildings;
* meaningful objects;
* traces of the player's history.

---

# 7. Foundational World Rule

All players begin with an equivalent foundational Personal Island.

The island is therefore fair as a starting property.

However, players gradually transform it differently.

Two players may begin with essentially the same foundational space and eventually produce completely different islands.

One may create:

* extensive farmland;
* barns;
* orchards;
* workshops;
* storage;
* animal areas.

Another may create:

* a small garden;
* forest;
* ponds;
* paths;
* decorative structures;
* a beautiful home.

Both are valid expressions of Underhallow.

---

# 8. Starting Property

The player inherits:

> **A small house surrounded by undeveloped land.**

The property is inherited from the player's deceased grandparent, consistent with the Narrative and World Bibles.

The inheritance initially appears ordinary.

The player receives:

* the house;
* the surrounding property;
* basic usable land;
* enough resources or equipment to begin;
* access to the basic farming loop.

The property is not presented as a broken ruin.

It is an old, functional home with visible history.

---

# 9. Starting House Condition

The inherited house should be:

> **Functional but visibly old.**

It should communicate that:

* someone lived here;
* the property has been cared for;
* the player has inherited something meaningful;
* improvement is possible.

The house should not require immediate restoration simply to become usable.

This ensures that the player's first emotional relationship with the property is:

> **“This is home.”**

rather than:

> **“This is another repair quest.”**

---

# 10. Starting Island Geography

The Personal Island should possess a meaningful natural identity.

It should not be a featureless rectangular plot.

The initial island may contain combinations of:

* meadow;
* woodland;
* small hills;
* streams;
* ponds;
* natural clearings;
* rock formations;
* shoreline;
* natural paths;
* existing vegetation.

The exact geography should be authored individually rather than generated as arbitrary terrain.

The island should feel like a naturally occurring piece of Underhallow.

---

# 11. Relationship to the Main Island

The Personal Island is geographically part of Underhallow's wider world, but it is spatially separated from the Main Island.

Travel occurs through the established boat/dock system.

The Personal Island should therefore feel:

* connected;
* accessible;
* private;
* distinct.

It is not a separate universe.

It is a place within the same world.

---

# 12. Initial Usable Area

The player begins with a **small usable area** surrounding the house.

Most of the island remains undeveloped.

This creates a clear long-term transformation:

> **Small home → growing homestead → developed property → personal estate**

The starting area must be large enough to support the basic gameplay loop without making expansion feel unnecessary.

---

# 13. Expansion Philosophy

Island expansion is a major long-term progression system.

Expansion should feel like:

> **developing a real property**

rather than:

> **unlocking additional inventory slots.**

New areas should physically become part of the player's usable island.

The player should be able to remember:

> “That forest used to be inaccessible.”

or:

> “That path wasn't here when I arrived.”

---

# 14. Expansion Methods

Personal Island expansion may use a combination of:

* progression;
* resources;
* currency;
* quests;
* restoration;
* clearing;
* discoveries;
* world relationships.

No single method should control every expansion.

Different sections may become available for different reasons.

The player should understand why an area has become available.

---

# 15. Physical Development

Expansion should be physically represented.

Examples include:

* clearing vegetation;
* restoring paths;
* repairing bridges;
* opening gates;
* removing obstructions;
* restoring abandoned structures;
* improving terrain access;
* developing previously unused areas.

Expansion should therefore create visible changes to the island.

---

# 16. Grid Structure

The Personal Island uses a **grid-based development architecture**.

The grid provides the structural foundation for:

* development areas;
* farming;
* major building placement;
* spatial organization;
* persistent state;
* future multiplayer synchronization.

However:

> **The grid is a development structure, not the visual identity of the island.**

The player should experience the island as a physical landscape rather than a spreadsheet of tiles.

---

# 17. Development Areas

The island is divided into unlockable development areas.

These areas provide structure while preserving player agency.

An unlocked area may support combinations of:

* farmland;
* orchards;
* pasture;
* buildings;
* workshops;
* storage;
* gardens;
* decorative space;
* paths;
* woodland;
* natural habitat.

The player should generally determine the purpose of an unlocked area.

---

# 18. Player Choice

The Personal Island should support substantial player agency.

The player should decide:

> “What should this part of my island become?”

Where world constraints permit, the player can choose between different uses.

This means that Personal Island progression is not simply:

> Unlock Plot A → Build Farm A.

Instead:

> Unlock Area A → Decide what Area A becomes.

---

# 19. Preserved Wilderness

The player should not be required to develop every part of the island.

Significant wilderness should remain possible.

Players may deliberately preserve:

* forests;
* meadows;
* ponds;
* wildflower fields;
* natural rock formations;
* shoreline;
* wildlife habitat.

An undeveloped area is not automatically an unfinished area.

Preserving nature is a valid form of island development.

---

# 20. Farming Role

Farming is a primary Personal Island activity.

The island should provide sufficient space for meaningful farming.

However:

> **The Personal Island is not simply a farm.**

Farming should coexist with:

* building;
* animals;
* workshops;
* storage;
* decoration;
* natural spaces;
* exploration;
* personal expression.

A player should be able to create a highly agricultural island without forcing every player into that identity.

---

# 21. Farming Integration

The Personal Island provides the primary controlled environment for the Farming System.

Farming may include:

* tilling;
* planting;
* watering;
* harvesting;
* crop organization;
* orchards;
* specialized agriculture;
* livestock;
* processing;
* agricultural buildings;
* eventual automation.

The Farming System remains governed by FB-001.

PI-001 determines how those farming mechanics occupy and shape the island.

---

# 22. Farming Freedom

Players should be able to determine how much of their island becomes agricultural land.

Possible island identities include:

### Agricultural Homestead

Large fields, barns, orchards, storage, processing.

### Mixed Homestead

Moderate farming combined with workshops, animals, gardens, and natural areas.

### Small Garden Home

Limited agriculture with heavy emphasis on decoration and living space.

### Specialist Property

A player may emphasize a particular production or lifestyle.

The island must support all of these.

---

# 23. Building Integration

Building is a major Personal Island system.

All construction follows BI-001.

Personal Island construction may include:

* homes;
* house expansions;
* workshops;
* storage;
* barns;
* animal structures;
* farming structures;
* crafting structures;
* processing structures;
* decorative structures;
* paths;
* fences;
* lighting;
* landscaping;
* future specialized structures.

Construction must remain data-driven and authoritative.

---

# 24. Placement Model

Personal Island construction uses BI-001's hybrid placement model.

### Major Buildings

Use structured placement and snapping where appropriate.

### Furniture and Decoration

Receive greater placement freedom.

### Landscaping

Uses appropriate contextual placement rules.

The objective is:

> **Structure where structure improves usability; freedom where expression matters.**

---

# 25. Building and Natural Terrain

Building must respect the island's geography.

Players should not be able to erase the entire character of the island simply because they unlocked an area.

Certain:

* natural features;
* protected trees;
* waterways;
* landmarks;
* special terrain;
* narrative objects;

may remain protected.

This preserves the island's identity.

---

# 26. House as the Central Progression Symbol

The player's house is the emotional center of the Personal Island.

House progression should follow the established direction:

> **Small Cottage → Substantial Homestead → Impressive Estate**

The progression should feel organic rather than purely numerical.

Each stage should communicate:

* increased stability;
* increased capability;
* accumulated history;
* changing identity;
* increasing attachment to Underhallow.

---

# 27. House Exterior Progression

The exterior should visibly develop.

Progression may include:

* increased size;
* improved materials;
* architectural additions;
* expanded rooflines;
* porches;
* gardens;
* exterior decorations;
* functional additions;
* architectural customization.

The final result should remain recognizably the player's original home.

---

# 28. House Interior

Interior development is equally important.

Players should receive significant control over:

* rooms;
* furniture;
* storage;
* decorative objects;
* functional stations;
* collections;
* personal items.

The interior should feel lived in.

---

# 29. House as Memory

The house should physically preserve the player's story.

Potential objects include:

* gifts;
* photographs;
* trophies;
* tools;
* letters;
* quest objects;
* rare discoveries;
* collectibles;
* objects associated with NPCs;
* objects associated with major decisions.

---

# 30. Decoration

Personal Island decoration should be extensive.

Players may customize:

* furniture;
* gardens;
* paths;
* lighting;
* fences;
* outdoor objects;
* landscaping;
* decorative buildings;
* house appearance;
* interior arrangements.

Decoration is not merely cosmetic.

It communicates identity.

---

# 31. Functional and Decorative Balance

The Personal Island should support two legitimate design philosophies.

### Efficiency

> “I want the most practical island possible.”

### Expression

> “I want the most beautiful island possible.”

And, importantly:

> **“I want both.”**

No system should force one philosophy to invalidate the other.

---

# 32. Player Lifestyles

The Personal Island should support multiple lifestyles.

Potential identities include:

* farmer;
* hunter;
* fisher;
* builder;
* explorer;
* collector;
* decorator;
* trader;
* mixed-role resident.

The player does not choose one permanently.

Their island naturally reflects what they spend time doing.

---

# 33. Progression Identity

Personal Island development is a major progression layer.

Progression may include:

* new development areas;
* house upgrades;
* new buildings;
* improved infrastructure;
* new farming capabilities;
* animal facilities;
* storage;
* workshops;
* decoration;
* specialized spaces;
* environmental restoration.

The island should therefore visibly grow alongside the player.

---

# 34. Island as Player History

The Personal Island is a physical record of player development.

Early:

> House + small field + undeveloped land.

Middle:

> Expanding farm + workshops + animals + personalized spaces.

Late:

> Developed homestead + mature house + specialized facilities + preserved wilderness + accumulated memories.

The final island should communicate:

> **“Someone has lived here for a long time.”**

---

# 35. Wildlife

Wildlife should naturally visit Personal Islands.

The exact wildlife present may depend partly on the island's environment.

For example:

* preserved woodland can support woodland creatures;
* ponds can attract water-associated wildlife;
* gardens can attract smaller animals;
* developed agricultural areas can influence local species;
* preserved natural areas can increase habitat diversity.

Wildlife should contribute to the feeling that the island is alive.

---

# 36. Wildlife Attraction

Players may influence wildlife through environmental choices and structures.

Possible influences include:

* planting;
* water features;
* preserving habitat;
* animal-friendly structures;
* food;
* landscaping.

The system should remain ecological rather than becoming a creature-spawning menu.

---

# 37. Resource Regeneration

Personal Island resources use a mixed regeneration model.

Common renewable resources should recover naturally.

Some resources may:

* regenerate slowly;
* require environmental conditions;
* require restoration;
* be limited in local quantity;
* be intentionally persistent.

The island must never become permanently ruined because the player gathered too many ordinary resources.

---

# 38. Animals and Livestock

Personal Islands support livestock and domestic animals.

Animals contribute to:

* farming;
* resources;
* daily routine;
* atmosphere;
* player attachment;
* island identity.

Animals should feel like residents of the property rather than resource containers.

Where appropriate, individual animals should be recognizable.

---

# 39. Daily Life

The Personal Island should participate in Underhallow's normal time simulation.

The island changes through:

* morning;
* afternoon;
* evening;
* night.

It should have its own daily rhythm.

Examples:

* crops grow;
* animals move;
* wildlife appears;
* lighting changes;
* the house becomes illuminated;
* environmental ambience changes.

---

# 40. Weather

Weather affects the Personal Island primarily through atmosphere and selected gameplay interactions.

Weather may influence:

* crop watering;
* wildlife;
* lighting;
* ambience;
* visual presentation;
* certain environmental events.

Weather should enhance the feeling of living in a real place rather than functioning primarily as punishment.

---

# 41. No Stamina Dependency

Personal Island activities do not use a stamina system.

This includes:

* farming;
* building;
* landscaping;
* ordinary interaction.

The player's constraints instead come from:

* time;
* resources;
* inventory;
* progression;
* spatial organization;
* personal decisions.

This preserves the project-wide no-stamina invariant.

---

# 42. Travel Rhythm

The player should not be forced to travel between the Personal Island and Main Island constantly.

Travel frequency should be driven primarily by player intention.

A player may:

* leave for an expedition;
* return home to unload resources;
* spend several days developing their farm;
* travel into town for social activity;
* return home;
* spend extended time exploring.

The game should support different rhythms.

---

# 43. Preparation Hub

The Personal Island functions as the player's primary preparation space.

Before expeditions, players may:

* organize inventory;
* select equipment;
* store resources;
* prepare tools;
* manage crops;
* craft;
* process resources;
* prepare food/items where applicable;
* inspect their property.

This connects the island directly to the expedition loop.

---

# 44. Storage

Storage is a core Personal Island function.

Storage structures may expand the player's ability to organize:

* resources;
* crops;
* materials;
* equipment;
* collectibles;
* processed goods;
* other items.

Storage should support organization without turning the player into an inventory manager.

---

# 45. Crafting and Processing

Personal Islands can contain crafting and processing facilities.

These systems remain governed by their respective specifications.

The Personal Island provides the physical location where appropriate.

Possible structures include:

* workshops;
* processing buildings;
* agricultural processing;
* specialized stations;
* storage-linked facilities.

---

# 46. Personal Island Mystery

The Personal Island contains a small number of meaningful secrets.

It must remain primarily a safe and comforting space.

Therefore, mystery should be:

> **subtle rather than oppressive.**

The player may gradually notice:

* something unusual about the property;
* an object left behind by the grandparent;
* an unexplained structure;
* evidence of an older history;
* unusual geography;
* something that does not quite fit the ordinary story of the farm.

---

# 47. Connection to the Central Mystery

The inherited property is eventually revealed to have significance beyond ordinary farming.

The Personal Island should therefore become one of the pieces of the broader Underhallow mystery.

However:

> **The Personal Island does not contain the entire mystery.**

It provides evidence.

The Main Island provides additional evidence.

NPCs provide memories.

Ruins provide physical evidence.

The player's grandparent provides personal evidence.

The player gradually assembles the larger picture.

---

# 48. Mystery Intensity

The Personal Island follows the project's three-tier world balance:

> **Cozy surface. Mystical middle. Deep mystery underneath.**

Most of the time:

> Home.

Sometimes:

> Something is strange.

Eventually:

> This place has a history I never knew.

The mystery should never destroy the island's emotional safety.

This directly follows the project's creative invariant:

> **Never let the mystery destroy the cozy life. Never let the cozy life erase the mystery.**

---

# 49. Grandparent Legacy

The grandparent's history is embedded carefully into the Personal Island.

Possible traces include:

* journals;
* letters;
* photographs;
* tools;
* furniture;
* stored objects;
* hidden compartments;
* unusual notes;
* property records;
* objects connected to investigations.

These should initially appear ordinary.

Their meaning changes as the player learns more.

---

# 50. Narrative Recontextualization

Objects on the Personal Island should sometimes gain new meaning as the story progresses.

An object that initially appears to be:

> “Grandfather's old tool.”

may later become:

> “Evidence that grandfather had been investigating this place.”

This supports the narrative principle that revelations should recontextualize earlier experiences.

---

# 51. Safety and Defeat

The Personal Island is the player's safe return point.

The non-lethal defeat model returns the player to their Personal Island after defeat.

Permanent progression is never lost.

The island therefore represents:

> **the place where progress is secured.**

---

# 52. Multiplayer Visits

Personal Islands may eventually support optional visits by other players.

The primary purpose is:

* social interaction;
* showing builds;
* visiting friends;
* sharing creativity;
* future cooperative activities.

Visits should remain optional.

A player who never participates in multiplayer should still experience a complete Personal Island system.

---

# 53. Multiplayer Boundaries

Personal Islands are player-owned spaces.

Persistent multiplayer interactions must respect:

* ownership;
* permissions;
* server authority;
* construction rules;
* interaction permissions;
* persistence rules.

Visitors must not be able to arbitrarily:

* destroy structures;
* remove resources;
* alter permanent state;
* interfere with ownership.

All persistent changes must follow authoritative multiplayer rules.

---

# 54. Personal Island vs Guild Island

The Personal Island must remain distinct from the Guild Island.

### Personal Island

> **“This is mine.”**

Focus:

* home;
* personal expression;
* individual progression;
* private farming;
* personal history.

### Guild Island

> **“This is ours.”**

Focus:

* communal construction;
* shared activities;
* guild identity;
* collective progression;
* cooperative spaces.

Guild functionality belongs to the Guild Island specification and must not be duplicated here.

The Personal Island should retain natural beauty even when highly developed.

Development should not require:

---

# 59. Performance and Technical Boundaries

Personal Islands are persistent player-owned spaces.

Therefore, the system must account for:

* persistent state;
* authoritative building state;
* efficient loading;
* efficient saving;
* multiplayer visitation;

> **The game state is the truth. The renderer is the representation.**

The following are persistent Personal Island state:

* furniture;

* farming state;

# 62. Development Philosophy

The Personal Island should create a sense of gradual transformation.

The intended progression is:

```text
Inherited Property
        ↓
First Home
        ↓
First Farm
        ↓
First Expansion
        ↓
First Major Building
        ↓
Growing Homestead
        ↓
Personalized Property
        ↓
Established Home
        ↓
Mature Island
```

This is not merely mechanical progression.

It is the player's life taking physical form.

---

# 63. Anti-Patterns

The following should be avoided.

### 63.1 Farm Spreadsheet

The island must not feel like rows of optimized production tiles.

### 63.2 Empty Building Sandbox

Construction must exist within a believable world.

### 63.3 Mandatory Optimization

Players should not need to maximize crop output to have a successful island.

### 63.4 Total Terraforming

Players should not erase the island's natural identity.

### 63.5 Decoration Without Meaning

Decoration should contribute to identity, atmosphere, or memory.

### 63.6 Mystery Horror

The Personal Island should not become a horror environment.

### 63.7 Multiplayer Dependency

The island must remain valuable when experienced entirely alone.

### 63.8 Excessive Maintenance

Farming, animals, buildings, and natural systems must not create oppressive chores.

### 63.9 Permanent Irreversible Mistakes

Players should be able to redesign their property.

### 63.10 Feature Dump

Every possible system should not automatically be placed on the Personal Island.

The island must remain coherent.

# 64. Player Experience Targets

The player should think:

> “This place is small, but it's mine.”

They should establish:

* their house;
* first crops;
* basic storage;
* first decorations;
* initial routine.

---

## Mid Game

The player should think:

> “I'm actually building something here.”

They should have:

* expanded land;
* meaningful buildings;
* animals;
* stronger farming;
* personalized spaces;
* visible development.

---

## Late Game

The player should think:

> “I built a life here.”

The island should contain:

* mature house;
* developed spaces;
* meaningful infrastructure;
* personal decorations;
* preserved natural areas;
* accumulated objects;
* visible history.

---

# 65. 20-Hour Target

After approximately 20 hours, the player should:

* recognize their home immediately;
* have developed a meaningful portion of the island;
* understand the basic development systems;
* have begun establishing their preferred lifestyle;
* have formed an emotional attachment to the property;
* still have substantial room for development.

The island should feel established but far from complete.

---

# 66. 100-Hour Target

After approximately 100 hours, the player should ideally be able to look at their Personal Island and see:

* years of development;
* their preferred lifestyle;
* accumulated memories;
* meaningful structures;
* personal decoration;
* natural areas;
* animals;
* farming history;
* progression history.

The island should feel deeply familiar.

But it should still contain room for:

* redesign;
* new projects;
* new decorations;
* new discoveries;
* new buildings;
* future systems.

---

# 67. Relationship to Player Identity

The player character does not receive a predetermined professional identity.

Instead, their identity emerges from what they do.

The Personal Island becomes one of the strongest expressions of that identity.

A player may look at their island and be recognized as:

> “A farmer.”

or:

> “A builder.”

or:

> “That person who has basically turned their island into a forest.”

or:

> “The collector with all the strange things in their house.”

This is desirable.

---

# 68. Core Island Philosophy

The Personal Island should embody five principles:

### 1. Home

It is somewhere the player belongs.

### 2. Growth

It visibly develops over time.

### 3. Choice

The player determines what it becomes.

### 4. Memory

The island records the player's life.

### 5. Mystery

Even home has a history.

---

# 69. Final Design Statement

> **The Personal Island of Underhallow should feel like a home the player slowly creates and grows into rather than a farm they are simply trying to optimize.**

It begins as inherited land.

It becomes a house.

The house becomes a homestead.

The homestead becomes a life.

And eventually, the player may realize that even the place they thought was simply home has a story of its own.

---

# 70. Canonical Personal Island Philosophy

> **“The Personal Island is where the player turns progress into place.”**

The Main Island gives the player somewhere to explore.

The Personal Island gives the player somewhere to belong.

The Main Island asks:

> **“What is out there?”**

The Personal Island answers:

> **“This is mine.”**

And beneath both is the larger philosophy of Underhallow:

> **Home can still be home even when you don't understand everything beneath it.**
