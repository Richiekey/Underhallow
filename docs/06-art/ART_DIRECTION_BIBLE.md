# 1. Purpose

The Underhallow Art Direction Bible defines the visual identity, artistic principles, presentation language, and production standards of Underhallow.

It exists to ensure that the game feels like it was created by **one coherent artistic vision**, even when assets, environments, characters, effects, interfaces, and other content are produced by different artists or AI-assisted development agents.

The goal is not maximum visual complexity.

The goal is:

> **Simple, cohesive, recognizable, beautiful.**

Underhallow should look immediately recognizable as Underhallow.

---

# 2. Core Visual Identity

The definitive visual identity of Underhallow is:

> **Highly detailed modern pixel art built on a classic 16-bit visual foundation, presented through an isometric perspective and wrapped in a cozy medieval-fantasy world hiding strange and mysterious elements.**

The visual experience should combine:

* Isometric pixel art
* Cozy medieval-fantasy architecture
* Natural environments
* Warm, inviting lighting
* Strong environmental depth
* Distinctive biomes
* Detailed but readable pixel-art assets
* Stylized human characters
* Stylized wildlife
* Subtle mystical elements
* Increasingly strange environmental details
* A strong contrast between the peaceful surface and the deeper mystery

The game should evoke the emotional familiarity of classic farming, simulation, RPG, and adventure games without visually copying any specific title.

---

# 3. The Visual Promise

When a player first sees Underhallow, the immediate impression should be:

> **"This looks cozy."**

After spending time in the world:

> **"This place feels alive."**

After exploring:

> **"There is something strange here."**

Eventually:

> **"Wait... what is actually going on?"**

The visual direction must therefore support two simultaneous layers.

### Surface Layer

* Cozy
* Beautiful
* Peaceful
* Friendly
* Warm
* Natural
* Wholesome
* Inviting
* Adventurous

### Underlying Layer

* Mysterious
* Strange
* Unexplained
* Occasionally unsettling
* Ancient
* Curious
* Potentially dark
* Visually inconsistent in deliberate ways

The second layer should be discovered rather than announced.

---

# 4. Foundational Art Principles

## 4.1 Cohesion Over Quantity

A smaller collection of highly cohesive assets is preferable to a massive collection of inconsistent assets.

Every asset should feel like it belongs to the same world.

The following should share a common artistic language:

* Buildings
* Terrain
* Vegetation
* Characters
* Wildlife
* Creatures
* Props
* Items
* Effects
* UI
* Icons

---

## 4.2 Readability and Beauty Have Equal Priority

Underhallow should not sacrifice gameplay readability for visual complexity.

Important gameplay information must remain visually understandable.

Players should be able to distinguish:

* Walkable terrain
* Obstacles
* Interactable objects
* Buildings
* NPCs
* Wildlife
* Resources
* Paths
* Water
* Elevation
* Important landmarks
* Hidden entrances

Beauty should enhance comprehension rather than compete with it.

---

## 4.3 Detail Must Have Purpose

Pixel density should contribute to:

* Material definition
* Depth
* Character
* Atmosphere
* Environmental storytelling
* Recognition
* World identity

Detail should not exist merely because more pixels are possible.

The art direction therefore favors:

> **Intentional detail over visual noise.**

---

# 5. Camera and Perspective

## 5.1 Isometric Perspective

Underhallow uses a fixed isometric/3⁄4 perspective.

The world should create the illusion of three-dimensional space while remaining fundamentally pixel-art driven.

The camera should communicate:

* Height
* Depth
* Elevation
* Spatial relationships
* Building volume
* Terrain shape

---

## 5.2 Camera Rotation

### V1.0 Decision: No Camera Rotation

The player may zoom the camera, but the world orientation remains fixed.

Camera rotation is intentionally excluded from V1.0.

This provides:

* Consistent visual composition
* Easier pixel-art production
* Stronger landmark recognition
* Predictable building presentation
* Easier navigation
* Reduced animation complexity
* A stronger visual identity

Future camera rotation may be evaluated independently if gameplay demonstrates a genuine need.

It is not part of the V1.0 visual contract.

---

## 5.3 Zoom

Zoom should allow the player to appreciate both:

* The broader composition of the world
* The detailed pixel-art craftsmanship of individual locations

Zoom levels must preserve readability.

The game should never require excessive zooming simply to understand an interaction.

---

# 6. Pixel-Art Philosophy

Underhallow uses **modern, highly detailed pixel art grounded in classic 16-bit principles**.

The target is:

> **Retro at the foundation, modern in execution.**

The art should feel nostalgic without feeling technically primitive.

Important characteristics include:

* Deliberate pixel placement
* Strong silhouettes
* Controlled shading
* Layered environmental depth
* Distinct materials
* Carefully designed highlights
* Consistent perspective
* Cohesive outlines
* Purposeful texture
* Strong color relationships

Pixel art should never become blurry or painterly simply because the scene contains many colors or details.

---

# 7. Architecture

## 7.1 Architectural Style

Underhallow uses **cozy medieval fantasy architecture**.

Architecture should draw from recognizable medieval-world elements while remaining fictional and distinctly Underhallow.

Common visual elements may include:

* Timber framing
* Stone foundations
* Plaster walls
* Wooden construction
* Sloped roofs
* Chimneys
* Cobblestone streets
* Wooden fences
* Stone bridges
* Market stalls
* Barns
* Workshops
* Inns
* Small towers
* Farmhouses
* Mills
* Docks
* Harbor structures

The world should feel lived in rather than designed as a collection of fantasy set pieces.

---

## 7.2 Architectural Philosophy

Buildings should communicate the lives of their inhabitants.

A farmer's home should feel different from:

* A blacksmith's workshop
* A merchant's store
* An inn
* A wealthy resident's home
* A public building
* An abandoned structure
* An ancient ruin

Architecture is therefore part of storytelling.

---

# 8. Architectural Age

The world uses a **mixed architectural age**.

Not every building should look equally old.

The environment should contain a visual history.

### Newer Structures

* Recently repaired
* Cleaner materials
* Fresh timber
* New construction
* Less weathering

### Lived-In Structures

* Worn wood
* Repaired surfaces
* Faded paint
* Improvised additions
* Uneven construction

### Old but Maintained Structures

* Generational architecture
* Carefully preserved materials
* Old foundations
* Mature surrounding vegetation
* Signs of long-term habitation

### Ancient Structures

* Ruins
* Collapsed walls
* Overgrown foundations
* Forgotten paths
* Strange stonework
* Abandoned structures
* Architecture whose original purpose is unclear

Architectural age should therefore help communicate that Underhallow has a history that predates the player's arrival.

---

# 9. Player House

The player's house is one of the strongest visual symbols of progression.

The intended progression is:

> **Inherited Cottage → Substantial Homestead → Impressive Personalized Estate**

The exact upgrade sequence remains a gameplay/economy concern, but the artistic principle is fixed.

---

## 9.1 Early Game

The starting home should communicate:

* Humble beginnings
* Limited resources
* Potential
* Belonging
* Opportunity

It should feel like something the player can genuinely improve.

---

## 9.2 Mid Game

The home becomes a substantial homestead.

Possible visual developments include:

* Larger footprint
* Additional rooms
* Porch
* Improved roof
* Better materials
* Gardens
* Workshops
* Storage
* Decorative elements
* Landscaping

---

## 9.3 Late Game

The final home should feel impressive without becoming universally extravagant.

The goal is not:

> "The player has the biggest mansion."

The goal is:

> **"This place became theirs."**

Player expression should remain important.

Two players should be able to reach similar levels of progression while producing radically different homes.

---

Terrain may include:

* Hills

# 11. Environmental Density

Underhallow should use **moderately dense environments**.

Scenes should contain enough detail to feel alive without becoming visually overwhelming.

Environmental detail can include:

* Trees
* Bushes
* Flowers
* Rocks
* Fences
* Signs
* Barrels
* Benches
* Lamps
* Tools
* Market goods
* Farm equipment
* Paths
* Small architectural details
* Wildlife
* Water features

Empty space is intentional.

Breathing room allows:

* Peaceful traversal
* Better composition
* Landmark recognition
* Atmospheric moments
* Future expansion

---

# 12. Vegetation and Flora

Flora should be:

* Naturalistic
* Stylized
* Regionally appropriate
* Recognizable
* Cohesive with the pixel-art language

Different regions should have distinct vegetation.

A forest should not simply be the same trees with a different color palette.

Flora should communicate:

* Climate
* Geography
* Season
* Elevation
* Region identity

Rare or unusual flora may become part of the game's mystery language.

---

# 13. Water

Water is both a geographic and visual feature.

It should support:

* Rivers
* Lakes
* Coastlines
* Fishing
* Docks
* Boats
* Harbors
* Traversal
* Atmospheric composition

Water should feel alive through:

* Surface movement
* Reflections
* Lighting
* Shore transitions
* Ripples
* Weather interaction

Water should also contribute to the world's sense of depth.

---

# 14. Lighting

**Cozy lighting is one of Underhallow's primary visual signatures.**

Lighting should make ordinary activities feel pleasant.

Important lighting states include:

* Morning
* Midday
* Afternoon
* Sunset
* Evening
* Night

---

## 14.1 Daytime

Daytime should favor:

* Natural light
* Warm highlights
* Clear environmental readability
* Soft shadows
* Strong color separation

---

## 14.2 Sunset

Sunset is an opportunity for especially beautiful compositions.

The world may shift toward:

* Warm oranges
* Golden yellows
* Softer shadows
* Deeper environmental contrast

---

## 14.3 Night

Night should become more atmospheric rather than simply darker.

Visual elements may include:

* Deep purples
* Blue shadows
* Warm windows
* Lanterns
* Moonlight
* Reflections
* Firelight
* Silhouettes

Night should sometimes feel peaceful.

At other times, it should make the world feel slightly unfamiliar.

---

# 15. Weather

Weather is primarily atmospheric, with occasional gameplay consequences.

Visual weather may include:

* Rain
* Cloud cover
* Wind
* Storms
* Fog
* Other future conditions

Weather should influence:

* Lighting
* Ambient effects
* Water
* Vegetation
* NPC behavior
* Wildlife behavior
* Farming presentation
* Exploration atmosphere

Weather should never become visual clutter.

---

# 16. Characters

## 16.1 Character Proportions

Characters should use:

> **Detailed pixel humans with clearly stylized proportions.**

They should remain recognizable as people rather than becoming fully chibi or heavily exaggerated.

Stylization should appear through:

* Proportions
* Clothing
* Hair
* Silhouettes
* Facial construction
* Animation
* Color design

This permits a wide range of:

* Ages
* Body types
* Professions
* Personalities
* Clothing styles
* Social identities

---

# 17. Character Silhouettes

NPC silhouettes should be sufficiently distinct to aid recognition.

Silhouette differences may come from:

* Hairstyles
* Hats
* Clothing
* Tools
* Body shape
* Posture
* Accessories

Major characters should have especially recognizable silhouettes.

This becomes important as the player encounters more inhabitants and begins recognizing them before reading their names.

---

# 18. Character Animation

Character animation uses a tiered approach.

### Ordinary NPCs

Moderate animation:

* Walking
* Idling
* Working
* Sitting
* Eating
* Farming
* Talking
* Basic reactions

### Major Characters

Highly expressive animation:

* Unique idle behavior
* Distinctive gestures
* Emotional reactions
* Story-specific animations
* Character-specific movement
* Stronger body language

The objective is to make important characters feel significantly more alive without requiring every NPC to receive the same animation budget.

---

# 19. Character Expression

Expression should primarily communicate through:

* Body language
* Posture
* Movement
* Gestures
* Facial expression
* Animation timing

Major emotional moments may temporarily break from the ordinary animation language when appropriate.

The visual direction should support the game's character-driven narrative philosophy.

NPCs are inhabitants first and quest dispensers second.

---

# 20. Wildlife

Wildlife uses a **stylized-realistic visual language**.

Animals should remain immediately recognizable while belonging to Underhallow's artistic universe.

The hierarchy is:

> **Real-world inspiration → stylization → occasional fantasy.**

Ordinary wildlife should not look like fantasy monsters.

Potential wildlife includes:

* Deer
* Rabbits
* Boars
* Wolves
* Birds
* Fish
* Other regional animals

Each species should have recognizable silhouettes and behavior.

---

# 21. Dangerous Creatures

Underhallow uses a mixture of:

* Natural wildlife
* Slightly fantastical creatures
* Genuinely strange/magical creatures

The majority of encounters should remain grounded.

Fantasy should become more prominent as the player moves deeper into unusual parts of the world.

This creates a visual progression:

> **Normal → Unusual → Strange → Impossible**

This progression is important to the game's mystery.

---

# 22. Mystery Creature Visual Language

Mystery creatures should be visually distinct, but **subtly so**.

They should not immediately resemble generic fantasy monsters.

Possible characteristics include:

* Unusual anatomy
* Slightly incorrect proportions
* Strange movement
* Unnatural coloration
* Unusual eyes
* Biological features that don't make sense
* Behavior inconsistent with ordinary wildlife
* Subtle environmental effects
* Familiar forms with unfamiliar characteristics

The intended player reaction is:

> **"I've never seen anything like that."**

Not:

> **"That's obviously the evil monster."**

Mystery creatures should feel as though they are connected to something deeper.

---

# 23. Mystical Elements

Magic is rare.

Therefore, mystical visual elements should carry weight.

They should not saturate every environment.

Potential manifestations include:

* Strange plants
* Unusual natural phenomena
* Ancient symbols
* Unexplained lights
* Impossible environmental formations
* Strange creatures
* Objects behaving incorrectly
* Places that appear subtly different
* Other unexplained phenomena

Mystical elements should feel **special because they are uncommon**.

---

# 24. Environmental Storytelling

Environmental storytelling is a major part of Underhallow's visual language.

The world should sometimes communicate information without dialogue.

Examples:

* Abandoned buildings
* Strange foundations
* Damaged structures
* Old paths
* Unusual markings
* Forgotten objects
* Overgrown ruins
* Objects placed somewhere they shouldn't be
* Buildings that appear older than the surrounding settlement
* Areas that seem deliberately avoided
* Environmental changes associated with the deeper mystery

The player should be able to discover stories simply by paying attention.

---

# 25. The Visual Mystery Curve

The visual world should gradually change in how strange it feels.

### Early Game

The world is overwhelmingly cozy.

Mystery is barely visible.

### Early Discovery

Small anomalies appear.

The player may notice something unusual but dismiss it.

### Growing Awareness

Patterns begin emerging.

Certain locations, creatures, objects, or architectural elements feel connected.

### Revelation

The player begins understanding that the strange details are not isolated accidents.

### Major Mystery

The visual language of the mystery becomes more apparent.

However, the cozy world does not disappear.

This contrast is fundamental.

---

# 26. Color Palette

The core palette is built around:

* Natural greens
* Warm yellows
* Earthy browns
* Soft blues
* Pastel colors
* Sunset oranges
* Deep nighttime purples

Colors should generally feel warm, natural and inviting.

The palette may become more unusual in locations associated with the deeper mystery.

However, mystery colors should still feel like part of Underhallow rather than a completely different game.

---

# 27. Biome Color Identity

Biomes must be visually distinct.

Distinctiveness may come from combinations of:

* Palette
* Vegetation
* Terrain
* Architecture
* Lighting
* Wildlife
* Weather
* Environmental props

Biomes should never feel like recolored versions of one another.

---

# 28. UI Direction

The Underhallow UI uses:

> **Modern usability + pixel-art styling + handcrafted/storybook personality.**

The interface should feel like part of the world without pretending to be an antique game menu.

---

## 28.1 UI Characteristics

The UI should favor:

* Clean hierarchy
* Strong readability
* Pixel-art visual language
* Warm materials
* Handcrafted details
* Restrained ornamentation
* Clear interaction states
* Smooth presentation

Potential visual materials include:

* Wood
* Paper
* Parchment
* Stone
* Cloth
* Painted surfaces

These are visual inspirations rather than mandatory literal textures.

---

# 29. Typography

The typography system uses:

> **Pixel-art headings + highly readable modern body typography.**

Large titles and important labels may use distinctive pixel typography.

Dialogue, descriptions, menus and longer text should prioritize readability.

Typography must never make lore, dialogue or gameplay information difficult to consume.

---

# 30. Icons

Icons should use:

> **Handcrafted pixel-art iconography.**

Icons should feel like part of Underhallow rather than generic UI symbols.

They should prioritize:

1. Recognition
2. Consistency
3. Readability
4. Personality

Important icon families should share:

* Stroke/shape language
* Pixel density
* Shading conventions
* Perspective conventions
* Visual weight

---

Visual effects should reinforce the game's pixel-art language.

Effects may include:

Effects should remain readable and should not overwhelm the environment.

Props should make spaces feel inhabited.

# 33. Landmark Design

Important locations should have strong visual identities.

A player should eventually be able to recognize major landmarks from a distance.

Landmark identity may come from:

* Architecture
* Height
* Silhouette
* Color
* Lighting
* Terrain
* Vegetation
* Unique props

Landmarks are part of both visual identity and navigation.

---

# 34. Personal Island Art Direction

The Personal Island should feel like a genuine place, not an isolated farming interface.

Its visual identity should evolve with the player.

As the player develops the island:

* Land becomes more cultivated
* Buildings appear
* Paths develop
* Vegetation changes
* Decorations accumulate
* The house evolves
* Specialized areas emerge

The island should visibly tell the story of what the player has done.

Two players beginning from essentially the same foundation should eventually produce visibly different worlds.

---

# 35. Main Island Art Direction

The Main Island should feel authored but naturally occurring.

It should contain:

* Town
* Residential areas
* Markets
* Farms
* Forests
* Wilderness
* Coastline
* Harbor
* Hidden locations
* Story locations
* Landmarks
* Resource areas
* NPC homes and workplaces

The Main Island should feel like one connected place rather than a collection of unrelated zones.

---

# 36. Cozy vs. Strange

The central art-direction tension is:

> **Comfort on the surface. Mystery underneath.**

The strange elements should generally be more effective when contrasted against normal beauty.

A beautiful forest is more unsettling when one part of it doesn't behave normally.

A warm village is more mysterious when everyone seems to know something the newcomer doesn't.

A peaceful farm is more intriguing when an ordinary crop behaves strangely.

The art direction should therefore avoid turning Underhallow into a permanently dark or horror-looking world.

---

# 37. Visual Signature

The six defining visual signatures of Underhallow are:

### 1. Cozy Lighting

Warm, atmospheric lighting makes ordinary life feel inviting.

### 2. Pixel-Art Density

Detailed modern pixel art provides visual richness while preserving the retro foundation.

### 3. Color Palette

Natural greens, warm yellows, earthy browns, soft blues, pastels, sunset oranges and deep nighttime purples establish the world's emotional identity.

### 4. Architecture

Cozy medieval-fantasy architecture gives the world a recognizable physical identity.

### 5. Mystical Elements

Rare mystical phenomena make extraordinary moments feel meaningful.

### 6. Strange Environmental Details

Small visual inconsistencies gradually reveal that the world is not what it appears to be.

---

# 38. The Underhallow Visual Rule

All future art should be evaluated against the following rule:

> **Would this look natural in the cozy world of Underhallow?**

If yes, continue.

If it is visually impressive but feels like it belongs to another game, reject or redesign it.

A second rule applies to mystery content:

> **Would this be intriguing because it feels slightly wrong, rather than obvious because it looks like a monster?**

If yes, it fits the mystery language.

---

# 39. Art Production Principles

AI-assisted and human-created assets must follow the same visual standards.

Agents and artists must not independently introduce:

* New perspective conventions
* Incompatible pixel density
* Unrelated color systems
* Completely different outline styles
* Unrelated character proportions
* Inconsistent lighting
* Modern vector aesthetics that clash with the world
* Excessive visual detail without purpose

Every new visual system should be reconciled against this Bible.

---

# 40. Asset Consistency

Assets should maintain consistency in:

* Perspective
* Pixel scale
* Lighting direction
* Shading logic
* Material rendering
* Color relationships
* Silhouette language
* Environmental density
* Animation principles

Consistency is more important than individual asset impressiveness.

---

# 41. What Underhallow Must Avoid

Underhallow should avoid becoming:

* Generic medieval fantasy
* Generic pixel RPG
* Dark fantasy
* Horror game
* Chibi farming simulator
* Hyper-realistic pixel art
* Visually noisy pixel art
* Oversaturated fantasy
* UI-heavy simulation software
* A collection of disconnected asset packs

The game should remain unmistakably its own thing.

---

# 42. Final Art Direction Statement

Underhallow is a **cozy medieval-fantasy world rendered in highly detailed modern pixel art through a fixed isometric perspective**.

It should initially feel warm, beautiful, peaceful and familiar.

Its architecture should feel lived in.

Its landscapes should feel naturally formed.

Its characters should feel like real people expressed through stylized pixel art.

Its wildlife should feel recognizable but capable of becoming strange.

Its mystical elements should remain rare.

Its visual details should reward attention.

And beneath the beauty, there should always be the possibility that something is not quite right.

The player's first impression should be:

> **"I want to live here."**

The player's later realization should be:

> **"I need to understand this place."**

The ultimate visual identity of Underhallow is therefore:

> **A beautiful cozy world that becomes increasingly difficult to explain.**

And the central artistic rule remains:

> **Never let the mystery destroy the cozy life. Never let the cozy life erase the mystery.**
